## What source-checked means

For the 25 additions, the linked dictionary text supports the selected lexical sense. Headword vowels, root, pattern, English gloss and sentence usage received an agent editorial assessment. Patterns are editorial morphological analyses, not a claim that every dictionary explicitly lists a pattern. The new example and everyday context are original sentences written for this product. They are not historical quotations or corpus attestations. English translations are editorial.

No new literary or religious quotation is introduced. The earlier hadith excerpts for IDs 3 and 5 retain their precise source records. A future human Arabic editor should review the eligible pool, including the English glosses and morphology. No native-speaker certification, measured contemporary frequency analysis, or full poetry-edition comparison is claimed.

## Corrections and selection

ID 6 now distinguishes الصَّبَابَة, tender longing, from الصُّبَابَة, liquid left in a vessel. Its old vowel explanation incorrectly put a shadda on the first bāʾ. The new example has no historical attribution.

ID 30 no longer presents a stitched quotation as Imru al-Qays. The opening belongs to Zuhayr's Muʿallaqa, while the added rain phrase was not established as part of that text. The replacement is an original sentence demonstrating الجَوْد, abundant rain. It does not invent a revised attribution. The vocalized dictionary text supports الجَوْد for rain and separately gives الجُود for generosity.

ID 160 now gives a general connection or relationship sense. Its pattern is عِلَة because the initial root wāw is deleted. The 25 additions remove inflated claims such as perfect mastery, unerring judgment, and a necessary religious frame for ordinary understanding or relief.

Existing original extension examples for IDs 24, 25, 41 and 46 now also live in the shared source. This removes differences between `example` and `exampleAr`. Their lexical status remains pending. ID 32 receives the newly checked original example in the shared source.

Daily usefulness now favors concepts applicable to studying, work, relationships and ordinary observation: understanding, learning, care, patience, honest speech, resolve, prudent planning and weather. The register and usefulness labels are editorial choices, not measured frequency claims. Rare praise words remain available in the checked pool with low usefulness. IDs and historical inventory remain stable.

## Pending risks and next review batches

The ledger records individual flags where the screen found a specific concern. Unflagged pending entries are not implicitly approved.

- Check quoted poetry and prose against a primary edition. A familiar phrase or famous author name does not establish attribution. High-priority examples include IDs 9, 22, 26, 117, 206, 225, 229, 249, 278 and 295.
- Check vowel-sensitive senses and patterns. Prioritize IDs 49, 58, 224, 299 and 352.
- Remove unsupported domain claims. Prioritize the immediate-death claim in ID 27, the uniqueness claim in ID 345, the fixed duration in ID 270, and the favorable framing of الفتك in ID 307.
- Check religious excerpts against exact references and ensure the cited sense matches the gloss. ID 107 currently illustrates اليقين with a verse commonly interpreted as death. Hadiths need precise references and appropriate authenticity context.
- Expand practical vocabulary in complete batches with lexical sources and original examples. The present inventory overweights literary praise, romantic longing and near-synonyms. Add variety before claiming a full year of useful daily learning.

The 30-entry selection pool is a bounded launch subset. It cannot provide 365 independently reviewed daily words. Do not market this audit as a completed year-long collection review. Repeated sampling from this pool remains possible after its variety is exhausted.
